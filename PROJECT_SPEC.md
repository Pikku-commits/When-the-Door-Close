# Build an Interactive Anonymous Scrapbook Website — "When the Door Close"

## 1. Project concept

Create a fully functional, immersive, vintage-inspired anonymous digital journal named **When the Door Close**.

The website should feel like discovering an old, mysterious journal in a quiet corner of a café, filled with handwritten thoughts, personal stories, photographs, opinions, unsent letters and memories left behind by different strangers.

It should provide a peaceful space where people can express themselves freely without the pressure of social media, personal identities, followers or popularity.

The website should combine three emotional aesthetics:

- Soft, romantic and nostalgic.
- Dark academia and intellectual.
- Cozy, playful and comforting.

The experience should feel deeply human, artistic, imperfect and emotionally meaningful.

Do not design it like a conventional blog, social media platform, corporate dashboard or generic modern website. The website itself must look and behave like a real, physical scrapbook and journal.

## 2. Website identity

**Website name:** When the Door Close

**Primary tagline:**

When the door closes, the world grows quiet, and the words finally find their way onto paper.

**Secondary tagline:**

No names. No faces. Just little pieces of being human.

Use the exact website name throughout the experience.

## 3. Opening screen and book cover

When a visitor opens the website, display a beautiful closed antique brown leather journal resting on a dark wooden surface.

Include:

- Realistic aged leather textures.
- Slightly worn book corners.
- Antique gold decorative borders.
- Embossed website title.
- A burgundy-red silk ribbon bookmark hanging from the pages.
- Warm, soft lighting.
- Subtle moving dust particles.
- Natural shadows.
- A quiet, mysterious atmosphere.

Display the following message:

"Dear Stranger,

Perhaps you came here with something to say.
Perhaps you came here simply to listen.
Either way, there is a page waiting for you."

Add an elegant interactive button:

**Open the Journal**

When clicked, animate the book opening realistically, revealing the first page.

Include a subtle page-turning sound, with an option to mute it. Do not automatically play loud music.

## 4. Anonymous access system

The website must be accessible to anyone who has the link.

Strict requirements:

- No login page.
- No registration.
- No email verification.
- No account creation.
- No social media authentication.
- No requirement to provide real names.
- No public personal profiles.

Visitors should immediately be able to open and read the journal.

When someone wants to write or reply, ask them to choose a fictional pen name.

Display:

"Who would you like to be on these pages?"

Placeholder:

"Choose a name that isn't your own..."

Example pseudonyms:

- September Rain
- The Midnight Poet
- Paper Moon
- Nobody
- A Wandering Soul
- The Quiet Stranger
- Cinnamon Coffee
- Blue Butterfly
- Someone Somewhere

Allow visitors to generate a random poetic pseudonym.

Remember the chosen pen name locally on their device, but allow them to change it whenever they want.

Do not publicly expose device information, IP addresses or other identifying information. Explain that pseudonymity does not guarantee complete anonymity.

## 6. Realistic journal navigation

The main website must behave like a physical book.

On desktop, display two open journal pages side by side.

On mobile, display one page at a time with natural swipe navigation.

Include:

- Realistic page-turning animations.
- Clickable page corners.
- Swipe gestures.
- Keyboard arrow navigation.
- Handwritten page numbers.
- Table of contents.
- Chapter navigation.
- Jump to the latest entry.
- Jump to a random entry.
- Search by topic, chapter or pseudonym.
- Close the book and return to the cover.

### Red ribbon bookmark system

This is an essential visual feature.

Every page containing published entries should have a small red bookmark indicator visible along the edge of the book.

Empty pages should not display the same indicator.

Include a main red silk ribbon marking the last page the visitor read.

When a visitor clicks a bookmark, smoothly turn to the corresponding page.

Allow visitors to save favourite pages locally using the red bookmark icon.

Remember their last visited page so they can continue reading when they return.

As more entries are published, the journal should grow naturally with additional numbered pages.

## 7. The first page

Design the first page like a handwritten welcome letter.

Include this message:

"Dear Stranger,

This is a little corner of the world where you don't have to be anyone in particular.

Tell us about a movie that made you cry, a song you cannot stop listening to, a place that felt like home, a book that changed something inside you, or a thought that arrived at three in the morning.

Write a letter you may never send. Leave a wish for someone who might never know it was you.

You don't need a real name here.

Just leave a little piece of your world.

And perhaps, somewhere between these pages, someone will understand."

Add three main handwritten navigation links:

- Read what others left behind.
- Leave a piece of yourself.
- Take me to a random page.

## 10. Shared and private pages

Provide two clearly separated writing modes.

**Shared journal pages:**

Entries are published to the common journal and can be read by anyone with the website link.

**Private journal pages:**

Entries are stored privately on the visitor's device and must never be uploaded to the public journal database.

Allow private entries to be edited or deleted locally.

Clearly explain that private entries are device-specific and may be lost if browser data is cleared.

Never expose private entries through public APIs, searches, chapter listings or other visitors' sessions.

## 11. Handwritten replies and margin notes

Instead of a traditional social media comment section, allow readers to leave handwritten notes in the margins of an entry.

Include:

- Anonymous replies.
- Replies using fictional pen names.
- Discussion threads.
- Small appreciation hearts.
- Thoughtful responses.
- Alternative opinions.

Display replies on small pieces of attached paper or as handwritten annotations.

Avoid public follower counts, popularity rankings, competitive leaderboards and engagement-based feeds.

Encourage meaningful conversations rather than competition.

## 12. Photo, video and Polaroid uploads

Allow visitors to upload photographs and short videos.

Display photos as:

- Vintage Polaroids.
- Taped scrapbook photographs.
- Postcards.
- Paper-clipped photographs.

Allow captions beneath each photograph.

Compress images appropriately, limit upload sizes and securely store public media.

Strip unnecessary image metadata, particularly GPS information, where possible.

Do not require anyone to upload personal photographs or reveal their identity.

## 14. Time capsule

Create a special section called "For Another Day."

Allow visitors to write messages that will become publicly readable only on a selected future date.

Display locked time capsules as sealed envelopes with a small handwritten opening date.

Include a countdown showing the remaining days.

Use server-side access restrictions to ensure locked messages cannot be retrieved before their scheduled opening date.

Once the date arrives, unlock the envelope with a gentle animation.

For private time capsules, store them locally and explain that local device-based locks are not tamper-proof.

- Gentle rain.

- Soft fireplace crackling.
- Paper turning.
- Subtle pen-writing sounds.

Provide a discreet sound control and volume adjustment.

Include a reduced-motion option for visitors who prefer minimal animation.

## 19. Backend and permanent storage

Build a real working application, not simply a visual prototype.

Use React or Next.js for the frontend, with appropriate animation libraries for realistic book interactions.

Use Supabase or another suitable persistent backend for shared entries, replies, media, daily questions, reports and time capsules.

Create appropriate database structures for:

- Journal chapters.
- Published entries.
- Fictional pen names.
- Replies.
- Media attachments.
- Daily questions.
- Daily question responses.
- Time capsules.
- Appreciation reactions.
- Content reports.

Implement automatic timestamps, pagination, efficient media loading and permanent shared storage.

Published content must remain available after refreshing the website and must be visible from other devices.

Do not rely exclusively on browser local storage for shared content.

For private entries, drafts, personal bookmarks and reading progress, use local device storage.

Allow authors to edit or delete their public entries without creating an account by using secure, unguessable entry ownership tokens stored on their device. Store only token hashes on the server.

Explain that clearing browser data may remove their ability to manage those entries.

Use secure server-side validation, restricted database permissions and protected storage policies. Never expose administrative keys in frontend code.

## 20. Community safety and moderation

Since anyone with the link can contribute without logging in, implement safeguards without disturbing the peaceful journal aesthetic.

Include:

- Report entry and report reply options.
- Spam prevention.
- Rate limiting.
- Safe media upload validation.
- Protection against malicious scripts.
- Server-side content validation.
- A secure administrator-only moderation interface.
- Removal of harmful, abusive or personally identifying content.

Visitors should not need accounts. The administrator interface must have separate secure authentication.

Include a discreet community guideline:

"Leave your thoughts, not your cruelty. Respect the strangers who share these pages with you."

Before publishing, remind users that shared entries can be read by anyone with the link and that they should avoid sharing sensitive personal information.

Prevent search-engine indexing by default, while making clear that link access is not the same as private access.

## 21. Responsive design and accessibility

Ensure the entire experience works smoothly on:

- Desktop computers.
- Laptops.
- Tablets.
- Mobile phones.

Maintain the realistic two-page book experience on larger screens and a single-page journal experience on smaller screens.

Ensure accessible text contrast, keyboard navigation, readable typography, proper form labels and reduced-motion support.

Do not allow decorative scrapbook elements to cover written content.

Long entries must remain readable without overlapping pages.

Deliver a complete, deployable website with:

- Functional page-turning animations.
- Persistent shared database.
- Anonymous pseudonym-based posting.
- Public and private journal modes.
- All ten chapters.
- Real media uploads.
- Handwritten replies.
- Spotify and YouTube attachments.
- Voice recordings.
- Interactive secret envelopes.
- Future-date time capsules.
- Daily questions.
- Calendar of memories.
- Red bookmark navigation.
- Search and random-page discovery.
- Automatic draft saving.
- Mobile responsiveness.
- Moderation and security protections.

Provide database setup instructions, environment variable documentation and deployment instructions.

Do not create non-functional buttons or placeholder features. Any sample entries should be clearly marked as examples.

The finished website should feel like a living book that slowly fills with pieces of different people's lives.

The central design philosophy is:

**People should forget they are using a website and feel as though they are turning the pages of a journal that belongs to everyone and no one at the same time.**
